Which one should you get?

If you're not sure where a mirror will help most, start with the leaning full-length mirror — no installation means no regret if you move it later. For a bathroom specifically, the adhesive acrylic option is the safer, shatterproof choice. If you want a wall that makes a statement rather than just reflecting light, the round mirror cluster does that job best. And if you've already decided you want a permanent, designer-looking piece, the arched metal-frame mirror is the one to spend a bit more on.

Frequently asked questions

Does a mirror actually make a room look bigger, or is that just a myth?

It's real — mirrors reflect light and create a sense of depth, especially when placed opposite a window or light source. Size and placement matter more than the mirror itself.

Can I hang a mirror without drilling into the wall?

Yes — several options on this list use adhesive mounting or lean against the wall instead of hanging, which works well for rentals.

What's the safest way to mount a heavy floor mirror?

Look for one with an anti-tip wall strap included, especially in a home with kids or pets, even if you're just leaning it against the wall.
