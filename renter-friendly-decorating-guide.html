How to Decorate a Rental Without Losing Your Deposit

Protect the deposit, not just the wall.

Renter-friendly decorating fails in two predictable ways: an adhesive product that reacts badly with a wall finish, or hardware loaded past what it's actually rated for. Both are avoidable with a five-minute test and a bit of restraint on weight limits. Here's what actually protects your deposit.

Test adhesive products on a hidden spot first

Textured walls, fresh paint, and certain wallpaper finishes can react differently to command-strip adhesive than a smooth painted wall does. A small test patch behind furniture or inside a closet — left for a day, then removed — avoids a visible mark showing up later on a wall you can't easily touch up. Relevant to our command hook guide.

Weight-rate everything before you hang it

Every adhesive hook and strip has a stated weight limit. Mounting a mirror or shelf near that limit, rather than well under it, is the most common reason renter-friendly hardware fails — both the fall itself and the wall damage that comes with it. As a rule of thumb, stay at 60-70% of the stated limit for anything that will hang for months rather than days.

Tension rods rely on friction, not just size

A tension rod that's slightly loose will slowly walk down a smooth doorframe over weeks. Sized correctly and installed snug, they hold curtains and light dividers reliably without a single hole — see our no-drill curtain rod guide for sizing by doorway or window width.

Remove adhesive products the right way

Pulling a command strip straight off the wall is what tears paint. The correct method is to pull the tab slowly downward, parallel to the wall, which releases the adhesive's grip without taking the wall surface with it. This single habit is responsible for most of the difference between a clean move-out and a deposit dispute.

Frequently asked questions

Will adhesive hooks damage painted walls?

Properly rated adhesive hooks, tested on a hidden spot first and removed by pulling the tab straight down (not outward), generally come off cleanly on standard painted drywall. Textured walls, fresh paint under 30 days old, and some wallpaper are the higher-risk surfaces worth testing first.

How much weight can a command strip actually hold?

It depends on the size and brand, typically from 1 lb for small strips up to 16 lb for the largest. Check the specific product's stated rating and stay comfortably under it — about 60-70% of the max — for anything hanging long-term.

Do tension rods work on textured or uneven walls?

They work best on smooth, parallel surfaces. On textured walls or slightly uneven doorframes, add rubber grip pads to the rod ends to prevent slipping, since the friction fit depends on consistent contact with both sides.
