Which one should you get?

If you just need corner seating that actually fits, start with the small L-shaped sectional — it's the simplest option sized correctly for a small room. If you're unsure which side the chaise should face, or the room's layout might change, the reversible version removes that risk. Apartments with tight hallways or doorways are better served by a modular sectional, since it solves a delivery problem the other options don't address. And if overnight guests are a regular thing, the sleeper sectional is worth the extra bulk because it replaces a guest bed you'd otherwise need to find room for separately.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a sectional will fit through my door?

Measure your narrowest doorway, hallway turn, and stairwell if applicable, then check the sectional's individual piece dimensions (not the total assembled size) — modular sectionals are the safer choice when any of these measurements are tight.

What's the actual size cutoff for a sectional to work in a small living room?

As a rough guide, staying under about 80–85 inches of total width leaves enough walking space in most small living rooms — many sectionals marketed as space-saving still exceed this.

Are sleeper sectionals uncomfortable to sit on day to day?

Quality varies significantly by model — look for reviews that specifically mention everyday seating comfort, not just the pull-out mechanism, since some sleeper sectionals prioritize the sleep function over daily comfort.
